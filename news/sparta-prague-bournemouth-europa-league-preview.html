Goal-shy Sparta face the Europa League's form team at Letná

Brian Priske's side have stopped scoring and lost ground at home, and now meet a Bournemouth team with a perfect European record.

Sparta Prague – AFC BournemouthEuropa League17:45 UTC

Match centre →

Key points
  • Sparta: 4 points from 3 Europa League games
  • Bournemouth: 3 wins from 3, goals 8-1, six straight wins
  • Sparta drew 0-0 at Bohemians and dropped out of second place

Sparta Prague go into matchday four of the Europa League league phase in the middle of a scoring drought. They have four points from their first three games and know that every point now counts if they want to stay in the race for the knockout rounds.

Their latest league outing summed up the problem: a 0-0 draw at Bohemians on 1 November that cost them second place in the Czech table. Priske was booked at the end for complaining about only three minutes of added time, and afterwards said he hoped referees would be "professionals like us" and analyse matches as carefully as the teams do. Czech reports note that Sparta have failed to score in several games since 18 October.

“I hope the referees are professionals like us.”Brian Priske, Sparta Prague head coach

Bournemouth arrive in a very different mood. They are one of five teams with three wins from three in the competition, with an 8-1 goal record, and they have won six in a row in all competitions, including 3-0 wins over AC Milan, at Manchester United and against Aston Villa, followed by 2-0 against Leeds.

There has also been talk in Prague that Erling Haaland, whose cousin Jonatan Brunes joined Sparta in the summer, might be in the stands. Haaland said on Canal+ that he hoped to come and watch a match soon, but nothing has been confirmed. Kick-off is at 18:45 local time at Letná; on Sunday Sparta travel to Plzeň, where they have won only once in their last 18 visits.
