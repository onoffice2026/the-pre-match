Moyes' Everton can climb to fifth – Lampard brings Coventry back to Goodison

Two wins over Newcastle in five days have lifted Everton to seventh; Coventry, written off after four straight defeats, could move up to 11th.

Everton – Coventry CityPremier League20:00 UTC

Key points
  • Everton 7th; a win lifts them above Liverpool into 5th
  • Grealish still out after a 'breach of club discipline'
  • Coventry: four defeats to start, now a win from 12 points

Friday Night Football has a storyline on both benches. David Moyes, reflecting on his long career before this landmark game, admits the club's cup run has everyone dreaming: Everton are in the Carabao Cup quarter-finals and would move above Liverpool into fifth with a win.

They arrive in form. Everton beat Newcastle twice in a week – 3-1 in the cup, 1-0 at St James' Park in the league – and they did it without Jack Grealish. The on-loan midfielder is still unavailable after what the club called a breach of discipline; Moyes says there is a way back for him, but not yet.

Frank Lampard returns to the club he managed in 2022 with a Coventry side that has changed its story. The Sky Blues lost their first four Premier League games without scoring, then recovered: they beat Sunderland 3-1 last weekend and a win here would put them level on 12 points with eighth-placed Bournemouth. 'I would like to think there is a higher ceiling,' Lampard says.

Our view: Everton at home are the side with momentum, and Moyes' teams rarely give much away. Coventry, though, have found goals again and will not sit back. A home win is the likelier outcome, but Lampard's side should make it a contest.
